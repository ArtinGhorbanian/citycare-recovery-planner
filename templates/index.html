      How it is calculated

      pressure = 0.35 x predicted overload risk + 0.25 x bed occupancy + 0.25 x ICU occupancy + 0.15 x equipment usage, then resilience index = 100 x (1 - pressure).

      70 to 100 is a strong operational position, 45 to 69.99 is pressured but manageable, below 45 is severe operational pressure. It is a transparent summary created for this project, not a medically validated score.

                      How this model is built and tested

                      • Chronological testing. The earliest 80% of the recorded times train the model and the latest 20% test it, so the test period is always in the future. No timestamp appears in both sets.
                      • No target leakage. The six output columns are never used as model inputs. Feeding those future columns back in produces an R² near 1.0 that means nothing.
                      • R² measures regression quality. 1.0 is perfect, 0.0 is no better than always predicting the average.
                      • MAE is the average error in the unit of the target, so an MAE of 5 patients means the estimate is on average 5 patients away from what really happened.
                      • Macro F1 gives equal importance to Stable, Warning and High Risk, so a rare status cannot be hidden by a common one.
                      • Decision support, not certainty. Predictions describe a likely operational picture and must be checked by qualified staff.

                      Observed status

                      One forest predicts five continuous targets together and a separate forest predicts the capacity status. Likely ranges come from the spread of the individual trees, which is a model spread and not a guaranteed confidence interval.

                      Important scientific and operational limitations

                      • The configured CSV contains timestamped dataset records and is not a live hospital connection.
                      • The repository's generated demo dataset contains synthetic scenarios and does not represent real hospitals.
                      • The validated prediction horizon is two hours.
                      • The model cannot confirm actual physical bed or ventilator inventory.
                      • Resource allocations refer only to the emergency reserve entered by the user.
                      • A lower-pressure hospital is only a possible coordination candidate, not confirmed transfer capacity.
                      • Predictions must be checked by qualified hospital and emergency-management staff.
                      • This is not medical diagnosis or treatment advice.
